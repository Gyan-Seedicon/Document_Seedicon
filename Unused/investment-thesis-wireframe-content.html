What changed, and why

①

The thesis statement itself is now built from a formula, not written from a blank page — this is the main gap versus Carta/VC Lab guidance. Right now the editor opens on "Investment Thesis Title…" and "Start writing your investment thesis…" with zero structure — the investor has to know the formula already. Carta frames a thesis as sayable in one or two sentences, and VC Lab's template (used as the standard reference by a majority of new fund managers) gives the exact fill-in-the-blank pattern: [Fund Name] is raising a [$size] [stage] fund to back [target sector/founders] in [geography], leveraging [edge] — with a target length of roughly 35–40 words. The wireframe turns that formula into six small fields (name, size, stage, geography, who you back, your edge) that auto-compose into the statement, which the investor can then hand-edit. This is also the piece every other part of the module depends on — it's what generates the public-link headline, the founder-facing match summary, and the one-line description GPs use when pitching LPs.

⑨

Thesis Stress Test added — checks the statement against known failure patterns, not just word count. Carta's guide names specific pitfalls: a thesis vague enough to be "untrackable" (e.g. "investing in innovation"), an edge generic enough that "anybody could go out and execute" it, and portfolio math that doesn't actually support the stated fund size. None of these are things a word counter catches — this section runs the statement against exactly those three checks and tells the investor which one is failing, instead of silently accepting whatever they type.

②

Fund Overview becomes real input fields, not a collapsed guidance accordion. Today "Fund Overview" is just a label you click to read advice, then go write about elsewhere. Fund size, structure, and horizon are numbers — capturing them as data (not prose) means they can drive fund math later instead of living unstructured in a paragraph.

③

Investment Focus becomes tags, not a paragraph. This is the highest-leverage change: your own PRD says thesis criteria should power Startup Pool filtering (F07). That's only possible if stage/sector/geography are structured tags the system can query — a sentence like "we like B2B SaaS" can't be filtered on.

④

Guidance moved inline, next to the field it explains. The current "What to Include" panel forces the investor to read guidance on the right, then scroll back to write on the left, holding it in memory. Putting the "what to write here" hint directly under the field removes that round-trip.

⑤

Portfolio Construction added as its own section — currently missing entirely. Check size, ownership target, and reserve split are one of the three pillars Carta's guide treats as core to a thesis, and your PRD explicitly calls this out as underused. Right now there's nowhere on the page to enter it at all.

⑥

Your Edge is called out as the most important section, with starter prompts. This is the part LPs and founders actually weigh most heavily — "why you, not another fund." Giving it named prompts (network / operational expertise / track record) instead of a blank box lowers the blank-page barrier for the hardest section to write.

⑦

Decision Criteria becomes a weighted checklist instead of free text. Written as prose today, it can't be reused. Structured as criteria + weight, the same list can later drive deal scoring in the Pipeline and a DD checklist in the Data Room — one thing written once, used three places.

⑧

Thesis Health meter replaces silent, ungraded guidance. The current page never tells you if your thesis is actually complete or too vague — a known failure mode Carta calls "the strategic but untrackable thesis." A visible completion score, plus warnings when focus is too broad, gives the investor real feedback instead of just reminders.
